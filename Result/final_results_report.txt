============================================================
FINAL CONCLUSION
============================================================

Reward shaping produced no measurable overall performance
improvement over the baseline DQN under the tested
experimental configuration.

Both approaches achieved:

25.0% average win rate,
2.50 average finishing position,
and 0.735 average reward.

However, reward shaping affected individual agents
differently.

Agent 2 improved substantially under reward shaping,
increasing its win rate from 24% to 37% and improving
its average finishing position from 2.59 to 2.05.

Other agents showed either smaller improvements or
performance decreases.

Therefore, the experiment suggests that reward shaping
changed learning behaviour but did not provide a
consistent aggregate advantage in this experiment.


============================================================
LIMITATIONS
============================================================

1. Only 100 training matches were used per experiment.

2. The Chef's Hat environment is stochastic, so different
   runs may produce different outcomes.

3. Individual agents showed noticeable performance
   variability.

4. Reward shaping did not consistently improve every agent.

5. The experiment compared one baseline DQN configuration
   against one reward-shaping configuration.

6. A larger training budget and multiple independent seeds
   could provide stronger evidence about the effect of
   reward shaping.
